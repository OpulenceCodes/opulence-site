How one outreach campaign booked 25 meetings

A media production company came to us with strong work and an unpredictable pipeline. One cold email campaign, run over about two weeks, produced 130 replies, 25 booked meetings and $38K in new pipeline. Here is what made it work.

The numbers first

  • 1,800 leads sourced
  • 130 replies, a 9% reply rate
  • 25 meetings booked
  • $38K added to the sales pipeline

1. The list was small and specific

We did not buy a list of 50,000 contacts. We sourced 1,800 decision makers who looked like the company's best existing clients. When the list is right, the email can be simple, because the offer is relevant before the reader finishes the first line.

2. The emails were short and had four parts

  • A specific hook. Something true about the reader's own company, not a generic compliment.
  • A bridge. One line that names the gap between where they are and what they could have.
  • A one-line offer. What we do about that gap, in plain words.
  • An easy ask. A question they can answer in a sentence, not a request for a 30 minute call.

No long introductions, no feature lists, no attachments. It should read like one person writing to another.

3. The emails actually arrived

The best email is worthless in a spam folder. Outreach needs its own properly set up sending accounts, a steady sending pace and regular checks on where messages are landing. This part is invisible when it works and fatal when it does not.

4. Every reply got a human answer

130 replies is 130 conversations. Some said not now, some asked questions, and some were ready to talk. Fast, personal responses turned 25 of them into booked meetings.

Is outreach right for your business?

It works best when you sell to other businesses, you can describe your ideal client clearly, and one new client is worth enough to justify a real conversation. If that sounds like you, outreach can give you something referrals never will: a pipeline you control.
