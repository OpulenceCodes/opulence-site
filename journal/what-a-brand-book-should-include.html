What a brand book should include

A logo is not a brand. A brand book is the document that keeps everything you publish looking and sounding like the same company, whether your team is two people or two hundred.

Why it matters

Without a shared guide, every new post, proposal and web page is a fresh guess. Colours drift, the logo gets stretched, and the tone changes with whoever is writing. Customers notice, even if they cannot say why. A brand book removes the guessing.

The eight sections every brand book needs

  • Who you are. Your positioning, mission, values and the promise you make to customers, in plain words.
  • Logo. Every version, the clear space around it, the smallest size it can go, and examples of what not to do.
  • Colour. Exact values for screen and print, how much of each to use, and which combinations are readable.
  • Typography. Your typefaces, the sizes for headlines and body text, and how they pair.
  • Imagery. What your photos should show, how they are treated, and what to avoid.
  • Voice and tone. How you write, with examples of on-brand and off-brand sentences.
  • Layout. The grid, spacing and any graphic device that makes your pages recognisable.
  • Applications. How it all comes together on real things: social posts, decks, business cards, email signatures and your website.

The section most brand books forget

Governance. Who approves changes, where the files live, and how freelancers and AI tools should use them. Without it, the book is out of date within months. With it, the book becomes a working tool.

PDF or web page?

A PDF is fine to start, but it goes stale the day something changes. A web-based brand system, one link with downloadable logos, colour values and rules, is always current, and everyone works from the same version. That is how we run our own brand.

How to tell if yours is working

  • A new team member can produce an on-brand post on day one.
  • Nobody asks where the logo files are.
  • Your website, proposals and social pages look like they came from the same company.

If any of those is a no, the gap is usually in the brand book, not in the people using it.
